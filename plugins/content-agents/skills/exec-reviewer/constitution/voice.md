# Voice hard rules

Source: CData brand voice skill (mktg-brand-voice). Condensed for review. Each rule has an ID for findings and feedback.

## Voice notes

- **VOI-001 Expert, not arrogant.** Show expertise through structure and causal explanation. If it sounds like it's trying to prove expertise, flag it.
- **VOI-002 Clear, not simplistic.** Start in the customer's problem, name the real constraint, explain how the system works, show why the design matters.
- **VOI-003 Human, not casual.** Direct address, clean "we/you" ownership, warmth from confidence and respect.

## Word and phrasing rules

- **VOI-004 Words to avoid** (use rarely; suggest a mechanism-grounded replacement): unlock, seamless, holistic, end-to-end, leverage, delve, tapestry, landscape (abstract), testament, pivotal, underscore, foster, cultivate, robust, revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-gen, best-in-class, powerful, solutionize, synergize, disrupt, empower, platform (as the claim), AI-powered (as the claim).
- **VOI-005 You-phrasing for value.** When describing what the reader gets, the reader is the subject ("With Connect AI, you can..."). CData can be the subject only for a unique mechanism.
- **VOI-006 Distanced attribution for pain.** Don't tell the reader what they're failing at ("You're struggling..."). Use "Many teams find...", "Often, engineering teams...", or "You may be dealing with...".
- **VOI-007 "We" sparingly.** Don't let CData narrate its own greatness sentence after sentence.
- **VOI-008 Contractions preferred.** "You don't need" over "You do not need".
- **VOI-009 Cut qualifiers.** very, quite, rather, somewhat, pretty much, a bit, essentially, basically, arguably.
- **VOI-010 Active verbs.** Flag passive constructions that bury the actor.

## AI-writing patterns (flag any)

- **VOI-011 Significance inflation:** "marks a pivotal moment", "reflects broader trends", "evolving landscape".
- **VOI-012 Superficial -ing tails:** "..., ensuring accuracy and fostering trust".
- **VOI-013 Vague attributions:** "industry observers note", "experts argue".
- **VOI-014 Framing tropes:** "at its core", "what really matters", "the real question is".
- **VOI-015 Signposting:** "let's dive in", "here's what you need to know".
- **VOI-016 Filler:** "in order to", "due to the fact that", "it's important to note that".
- **VOI-017 Generic upbeat ending:** "exciting times lie ahead".
- **VOI-018 Forced rule of three:** three items that aren't genuinely distinct.

## Style rules

- **VOI-019 Em dashes:** no spaces around them; no more than one or two per sentence.
- **VOI-020 AP style with Oxford comma.**
- **VOI-021 Exclamation points:** never for product promotion or CTAs; max one per piece.
- **VOI-022 Ampersands:** spell out "and" unless space-limited or part of a proper name (e.g. "Controls & Security" is a capability name and stays).
- **VOI-023 Headers:** no end punctuation unless a question.
- **VOI-024 Casing:** Title Case for the headline only; sentence case for subheads, table headers, and general industry terms.
- **VOI-025 Acronyms:** full lowercase term first, acronym in parentheses, e.g. "master data management (MDM)".
- **VOI-026 Numbers:** AP (spell out one to nine) in prose; numerals in emails, ads, social, tables.
- **VOI-027 On-premises:** never "on-premise"; "on-prem" only in blogs after first full use.
- **VOI-028 Dates and times:** "June 11" (no ordinal); "11 a.m.", "noon", ranges with an unspaced en dash.
- **VOI-029 Lists:** bullets when order doesn't matter, numbers for sequences; parallel structure; consistent punctuation; no trailing semicolons.
- **VOI-030 Paragraphs:** one idea each, two to four sentences, no walls of text.
